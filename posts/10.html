Why not buy a domain?

Buying a domain isn’t always necessary, especially if you're just starting out. While domains are usually pretty cheap, they still cost money every year, and that adds up over time.

If you're experimenting with projects, learning how hosting works, or just building for fun, paying for a domain can feel like overkill. There’s also the risk of buying a domain for a project you later abandon.

Free domains remove that pressure and let you focus on building instead of committing financially.

Why even get a domain?

Even if it’s free, having your own domain makes a big difference.

A custom domain looks far more professional than a default hosting URL. It’s easier to remember, easier to share, and gives your project a more polished identity.

It also gives you flexibility—you can connect it to platforms like GitHub Pages, Vercel, or Netlify, and move it around whenever you want without changing your public URL.

If you're building a portfolio or showcasing work, a domain (even a free one) is a solid upgrade.

How to get a domain

After signing up, the process is pretty straightforward:

  • Create your account and log in
  • Search for a domain name you like
  • Choose an available free domain option
  • Register it to your account

Once you’ve got your domain, the next step is connecting it to your project. Most hosting platforms will give you DNS records (like A records or CNAME records) that you just copy into your domain dashboard.

I’d also recommend using Cloudflare for managing your DNS, as it’s fast, reliable, and adds extra features like caching and security for free.

After that, it might take a bit of time to propagate (usually a few minutes to a few hours), and then your domain will be live.

That’s it—you now have a working domain without spending anything.
